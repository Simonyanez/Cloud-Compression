\begin{block}{The Challenge: Unstructured Point Clouds}
Point clouds are a fundamental structure for representing 3D geometry and attributes, but their density and irregularity demand high-efficiency compression \cite{zhang2014point}. Traditional Graph Fourier Transforms (GFT) rely on structural graphs where weights depend only on spatial distance \cite{ortega2022gsp}. However, attribute signals (like luminance) often follow manifolds that do not align perfectly with geometry, leading to suboptimal energy compaction.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{From Geometry to Attribute-Dependent Graphs}
Our framework introduces an \textbf{Attribute-Dependent Graph} ($\mathcal{G}_a$) that adapts to local signal trends. By computing an \textit{Attribute Motion Matrix} $M_{ij} = W_{ij}(y_i - y_j)$, we identify "sink" nodes in the gradient field where the signal concentrates. Adding \textbf{self-loops} to these sinks modifies the GFT basis vectors, aligning them with the local attribute trend and maximizing energy compaction \cite{pavez2017separable}.

\begin{block}{Learning the Dictionary: RD-Optimized Clustering}
To achieve high-efficiency adaptation, we learn a codebook of $K$ transforms. Assignments are driven by a \textbf{Rate-Distortion Lagrangian} $J = D + \lambda R$, rather than simple distance metrics. 
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Exploration:} \textit{Simulated Annealing} enables stochastic block-to-cluster assignments, preventing early convergence to local minima on the non-convex cost surface.
    \item \textbf{Refinement:} \textit{Differential Evolution} optimizes the transform parameters ($\vec{slope}, w_{sl}, \tau$) to minimize the collective RD-cost of each cluster.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{The Signaling Tax: A Theoretical Breakthrough}
A critical discovery in this project is the \textbf{Overhead Trap}. In small block sizes (B4, B8), the bit-savings provided by a superior adaptive transform are often smaller than the cost of telling the decoder which transform to use.

\vspace{0.5em}
\textbf{The Dilution Principle:} Signaling cost per voxel ($\text{BPV}_{\text{overhead}}$) is inversely proportional to block volume ($B^3$):
\begin{equation}
\text{BPV}_{\text{overhead}} = \frac{H(L_i | L_{i-1})}{B^3} \quad \text{\cite{shannon1948mathematical,sayood2006introduction}}
\end{equation}
By moving to **B16** and **B32**, we "dilute" this tax across thousands of points, finally allowing net compression.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Bridging the Gap: Smoothing and CABAC}
To reach true bit-rate reduction, we implemented \textbf{Spatial Regularization}. By adding a penalty $\beta$ for cluster-switching between Morton-ordered blocks, we force long "runs" of identical labels. These runs are then exploited by a **CABAC-Proxy** (1st-order conditional entropy), which captures the spatial correlation that standard memoryless entropy ignores.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Project Findings and Future Work}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Scale Inversion:} Small blocks (B4) offer the best PSNR but are bit-rate inefficient. Large blocks (B32) are the only path to true net compression.
    \item \textbf{Unit Discrepancy:} Training must use high $\lambda$ ($\geq$5.0) to overcome the numerical dominance of MSE and prioritize bits.
    \item \textbf{Symmetry:} High $\beta$ values ($>$1000) are essential to align the codebook selection with the capabilities of arithmetic coders.
\end{enumerate}
Future work will focus on **Skip-Mode signaling** for structural blocks and \textbf{Hierarchical partitioning} to combine B32 efficiency with B4 detail preservation.
\end{block}
