\begin{block}{Introduction}
Point clouds are a fundamental data structure for representing 3D objects and scenes. Due to their large size, efficient compression is crucial for storage and transmission. This work proposes a novel lossy compression scheme for point cloud attributes, which leverages graph-based transforms and rate-distortion optimization.

\vspace{1em}

\textbf{Key Steps:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Block Partitioning:} Morton ordering preserves spatial locality \cite{morton1966zorder}.
  \item \textbf{Graph Transform:} GFT concentrates energy in few coefficients \cite{ortega2022gsp,pavez2020region}.
  \item \textbf{RD-Optimized Clustering:} Minimize Lagrangian cost: $J = D + \lambda R$ where $\lambda = \alpha \cdot Q^2$ \cite{ortega1998rate}.
  \item \textbf{Entropy Coding:} RLGR for coefficients \cite{malvar2006adaptive}, Huffman for cluster indices.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{block}{Information Theory Context}
\textbf{Source Model:} Non-stationary graph signal with spatial and chromatic correlation \cite{ortega2022gsp}.

\textbf{Rate:} $R_{\text{total}} = R_{\text{coefficients}} + R_{\text{overhead}}$ where sparsity approximates entropy: $R \approx H(Q(\theta))$ \cite{shannon1948mathematical}.

\textbf{Distortion:} PSNR on luminance (Y) channel from MSE in transform domain.

\textbf{RD Optimization:} Lagrangian finds Pareto-optimal points on achievable (R,D) curve \cite{ortega1998rate}.

\textbf{Adaptive Codebook:} Vector quantization creates codebook of $K$ transforms \cite{pavez2017separable}. Trade-off: local adaptation vs. overhead ($\log_2 K$ bits/block).

\textbf{Key Insight:} Smaller blocks (B4) preserve local stationarity, enabling superior adaptation \cite{pavez2020region}.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Huffman Code Analysis}
Cluster indices entropy coded using Huffman \cite{sayood2006introduction}:
\begin{equation}
L = \sum_{i=1}^{K} p_i \cdot \ell_i \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{BPV}_{\text{overhead}} = \frac{L \cdot N_{\text{blocks}}}{N_{\text{voxels}}}
\end{equation}

$L$ (bits/block): B16 (fast: 1.00, med: 1.95), B8 (fast: 1.00, med: 1.63), B4 (fast: 1.00, med: 1.50).
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Rate-Distortion Results}

\begin{figure}
\centering

\begin{minipage}[t]{0.45\linewidth}
    \textbf{Analysis:}\\
    The impact of clustering depends strongly on block size.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{B4:} RD-Fast and RD-Medium both outperform the baseline. At 1.44 BPV, RD-Fast gains 0.05 dB with a 1\% bitrate drop; RD-Medium reaches 0.07 dB. Across all bitrates, RD-Medium remains slightly superior.
        \item \textbf{B8:} Gains are modest. At low bitrate (~0.81 BPV) both strategies add ~0.01 dB and reduce bitrate by ~1\%. At higher bitrates, PSNR decreases slightly (–0.01 to –0.02 dB) while still yielding small bitrate savings.
        \item \textbf{B16:} Clustering overhead dominates. Both methods show small but consistent PSNR losses (0.01–0.02 dB) with negligible bitrate reduction.
    \end{itemize}
    In summary, clustering is clearly effective for \textbf{B4}, with consistent gains. Benefits diminish with larger blocks, and RD-Medium offers the best overall tradeoff at the cost of higher complexity.

\textbf{Key Observations:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{B4:} Both methods achieve bitrate reduction and quality gains; Medium outperforms Fast.
    \item \textbf{B8:} Maintains bitrate reduction with minimal quality loss ($<$0.01 dB).
    \item \textbf{B16:} Diminishing returns with minimal bitrate gains and noticeable quality degradation.
    \item \textbf{Optimal:} B4 provides best rate-distortion performance for practical use.
\end{itemize}

\end{block}

\begin{block}{Conclusions}
Clustering helps only when blocks are small. For B4, both RD-Fast and RD-Medium reduce bitrate and slightly improve PSNR. For B8, gains are minimal but generally neutral. For B16, overhead dominates and performance drops.

Overall, \textbf{B4 gives the best tradeoff}, with RD-Medium performing the best.
\end{block}
