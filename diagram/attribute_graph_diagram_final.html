AttributeGraph Construction Methodology

An AttributeGraph adapts a purely structural graph to the trends of a specific attribute (signal) defined on its vertices. This is a key component of the adaptive GFT computation in the RD Clustering algorithm. The goal is to modify the graph structure to improve the energy compaction of the GFT for a signal that follows a certain trend, represented by a slope vector \(\mathbf{s}\).

Start: Structural Graph

A weighted graph \(\mathcal{G}_{struct} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}, W)\) where weights are based on inverse Euclidean distance.

$$ W_{ij} = \frac{1}{\|\mathbf{v}_i - \mathbf{v}_j\|} $$
→

End: AttributeGraph

The structural graph modified with weighted self-loops on specific "sink" nodes.

$$ W'_{ii} = \text{self\_loop\_weight} $$

Step 1: Define the Attribute Signal Trend

The process starts with a slope vector \(\mathbf{s}_k\) from a dynamic cluster centroid. This slope is used to project the 3D vertex positions \(\mathbf{v}_i\) onto a 1D attribute signal \(Y_i\), representing the expected luminance trend.

$$ Y_i = \mathbf{s}_k^T \mathbf{v}_i $$

Step 2: Calculate the Attribute Motion Matrix (M)

This matrix, \(M\), quantifies the "motion" or difference in the attribute signal \(Y\) between every pair of connected vertices. It is weighted by the structural graph's weights \(W\).

$$ M_{ij} = W_{ij} \cdot (Y_i - Y_j) $$

A positive \(M_{ij}\) implies the signal "flows" from \(i\) to \(j\). The matrix is normalized (e.g., by dividing by 255) to keep values in a consistent range.

Step 3: Identify Sink Nodes (S)

A "sink" node is a vertex that has many incoming signal flows from its neighbors. A sink vector \(\mathbf{S}\) is computed to quantify how much each node acts as a sink. For each node \(i\), we count how many of its neighbors \(j\) have a lower attribute value (i.e., \(Y_j < Y_i\)).

This count is then normalized. The implementation provides two normalization methods:

Standard Normalization:

The sink count is divided by the total number of neighbors (degree) of the node.

$$ S_i = \frac{\text{count}(j | M_{ij} < 0)}{\text{degree}(i)} $$

Min-Max Normalization (currently used):

The sink counts for all nodes are scaled to the [0, 1] range. This is the method currently used in the implementation.

$$ S_i = \frac{\text{count}_i - \min(\text{counts})}{\max(\text{counts}) - \min(\text{counts})} $$

Step 4: Add Self-Loops

Nodes with a high sink score are selected to have self-loops added. A self_loop_threshold determines which nodes are chosen.

$$ \text{Selected Nodes} = \{ i \in \mathcal{V} \mid S_i \ge \text{self\_loop\_threshold} \} $$

For each selected node \(i\), a self-loop is added to the graph by setting its diagonal weight in the adjacency matrix to a specific value, self_loop_weight. This modification changes the graph's Laplacian matrix, which in turn alters its eigenvectors and the resulting GFT, adapting it to the signal's flow.
